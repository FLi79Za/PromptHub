# Suno Prompting Reference — July 2026

Source: `Suno_Master_Knowledge_Base_Canonical_July_2026.docx`, Version 2.1, 2 July 2026. This is a PromptHub-local operational digest, not a replacement for the source document. The source document is the authority for future dated maintenance.

## Evidence and portability

Use four evidence labels when they matter: VERIFIED for current official documentation; STRONGLY SUPPORTED for stable observed practice without formal specification; EMERGING for mixed or model-dependent evidence; SPECULATIVE for unsupported claims. Style descriptions and Exclude are portable typed controls. Lyrics-field contextual prompting is useful but probabilistic. Account-specific Voices, Personas, My Taste, Magic Wand and Custom Models are dependencies, not portable text instructions.

## High-signal style construction

Prioritise the main genre or clearly ordered hybrid, then groove or tempo feel, defining palette, vocal delivery, production character and sonic mood. Use two to four instrument anchors that materially define the result. Separate identity, delivery and production language. Keep concise prompts readable and repeatable. A longer request should be a short producer brief, not accumulated adjectives.

GMIV—Genre, Mood, Instruments and Vocals—is a useful house completeness check, not an official Suno architecture. Repeating a genre at the end is an optional A/B test, not a documented attention law.

Portable style pattern:

`[genre/subgenre], [groove or tempo feel]. [two to four palette anchors]. [vocal delivery if needed]. [production character].`

Performance-directed pattern:

`Style: [genre], [energy], [feel]. Palette: [anchors]. Performance: Verse [delivery]; Chorus [delivery]. Band [time feel]. Recording: [production phrase]. Exclude: [unwanted elements].`

Use relative performance language such as restrained verse, wider chorus, sparse intro, dense string layering, behind-the-beat groove and tightly locked rhythm section. Treat exact bar counts, second counts and precise automation as unreliable.

## Lyrics and structure

Lyrics influence rhythm, phrase length, repetition and density as well as meaning. Write physically singable lines and match line density to tempo and delivery. Use clear section labels and concise performance cues. Shorter lines and breathing room suit slow ballads; short rhythmic units and controlled syllable density suit fast rap or dense pop; large choruses benefit from fewer words, repeatable hooks and open vowels. Repetition can strengthen a hook but can also cause unwanted looping.

## Exclusions and uncertainty

Use `Exclude:` for unwanted genres, instruments, ambience or production traits, using short concrete terms. A positive replacement can be clearer than repeated negative phrasing. Do not claim that punctuation, capitalisation, parentheses, JSON-like formatting or bracket tags have deterministic hidden semantics. Use familiar section labels for human clarity and describe their adherence as probabilistic.

## Current control-layer cautions

When a Voice is active, describe delivery, register, articulation, harmony behaviour and mic feel rather than attempting to recreate identity through adjectives. Personas and Custom Models require the receiving account dependency. My Taste/Magic Wand reduces reproducibility; disable it or record the expanded result for controlled tests. Use Replace Section, Alternates, Studio repair or comping for local failures rather than escalating prompt folklore.
